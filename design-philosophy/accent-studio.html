Turn 3

The quarter arc as a system

The same breakout run on 1B. One difference governs everything here: solid wedges stack into fields, but strokes join end to end into a continuous line. So the pairings below are not tiled copies — they are one path that keeps going, which is a closer match to how the logotype's bowls are drawn.

Reference the ids in chat: 3a · 3b · 3c · 3d

3A The pair — S, half, circle

Chained in alternating directions the quarters give an S, one unbroken stroke travelling down the page. Turned the same way instead, two close into the b's bowl and four close the circle. Unlike the solid, the seams vanish — the family reads as one line at different lengths.

3B The divider

Here the rule and the mark are the same stroke: the line simply turns a quarter and stops. The top one turns up out of the amber and fades away right, which is Nocturne's fading rule with a beginning. The second brackets a block on both ends.

Turn 2

The quarter disc as a system

1A at 0°, held as the resting position. Everything below is the same wedge rotated in 90° steps and repeated — no second shape is introduced anywhere in the set. That constraint is the whole point: the marks stay recognisably one family because they are literally one file, turned.

Reference the ids in chat: 2a · 2b · 2c · 2d

2A The pair — S and half

Two quarters, 180° apart, make an S. Two quarters at 90° make a half-disc — the b's bowl. Four make the circle. The pair is the smallest unit that reads as deliberate rather than decorative.

2B The divider

A hairline given a terminal. The quarter caps one end and the rule fades out of the other, which is the Nocturne rule treatment already — this just gives the live end something to start from. Two caps for a closed rule between sections.

The ticks become quarters at a fixed 0°. The active one doubles in size rather than changing shape, so the rail animates as growth, not as a swap. Still nothing clickable.

2D The scallop — repetition as a field

The same wedge repeated at one rotation, fading out — a run of arcs reading as a single ribbon. This is the one to use sparingly: it works as a corner treatment on the closing card, where a single mark would be too quiet, and nowhere else on a page this restrained.

Turn 1

Accent marks — Bauhaus primitives from the logotype

The logotype is a geometric grotesque: true circular bowls, flat terminals, one 45° diagonal in the k, and a stem weight of roughly a fifth of the cap height. Each option below is built only from those facts — no new curves invented. Stroke terminals are flat, not rounded, matching the letterforms.

Reference the ids in chat: 1a · 1b · 1c

1A Quarter disc

The solid primitive. Two flat sides meeting at a right angle, one true circular arc — the same figure the b's bowl cuts against its stem. Reads as mass rather than line, so it holds at small sizes where a stroke would disappear.
